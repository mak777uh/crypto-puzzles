Cicada 3301 Liber Primus

#Cicada 3301 #Cryptanalysis

Introduction

Cicada 3301 was the signature used for a series of public cryptographic puzzle rounds in 2012, 2013, and 2014. The puzzles mixed classical cryptography, steganography, books, music, physical clues, Tor hidden services, and OpenPGP-signed messages. The stated theme was the search for highly intelligent individuals, but the identity and purpose of the group remain unverified.

Liber Primus, "First Book", is the central unresolved artifact from the later Cicada 3301 material. It is a book of Gematria Primus runes, with some pages solved and many pages still unsolved. Community sources differ in page numbering, but modern solver summaries often split the material into LP1, where 17 pages are solved, and LP2, where 2 of 58 pages are solved and 56 remain unsolved.

Cicada material should be treated carefully. The last verified public warning says to beware false paths and verify OpenPGP signatures from key ID 7A35090F. This guide distinguishes verified Cicada messages from community analysis and uses source links for reproduced transcriptions and known solve methods.

Most of Boxentriq's cipher tools can be used with Liber Primus. When a tool has a language selector, choose Gematria Primus; it is available at the bottom of the language list. This switches compatible tools from the Latin alphabet to the 29-rune Gematria Primus alphabet, so you can test Caesar, Atbash, Vigenere, and autosolver workflows directly against rune text.

When a tool result is still shown as runes, Boxentriq can also expose a Translate runes button. Use it to translate Gematria Primus runes in the output into Latin letter values without leaving the tool workflow.

What Is Liber Primus?

Liber Primus is a rune manuscript written with the 29-symbol Gematria Primus alphabet. Some pages are direct rune-to-Latin transliterations, while others use shifts, reversed alphabets, Vigenere-style keys, prime streams, or unknown methods. That mix makes the book useful for both learning and active cryptanalysis: solved pages are reproducible examples, and unsolved pages still offer frequency patterns, repeated fragments, spacing, and mathematical constraints to test.

How the Pages Were Found

The 2014 Cicada path began with a public image and again relied on layered extraction. Solvers used OutGuess and OpenPGP verification, followed a book-cipher path, reached Tor onion pages, and encountered a William Blake collage tied to the next stages. Later material involved RSA clues, a growing hex string, and XOR/JPEG recovery before the first Liber Primus-related images appeared.

More runic pages were later released as a larger dump. This is why source names can be confusing: one source may call a page 56.jpg, while another "complete set" view may refer to the same content as 73.jpg. In the examples below, page identifiers follow the scream314/cicada3301 transcription and asset layout unless stated otherwise.

Solving Liber Primus With Boxentriq

Start by transcribing runes or pasting existing transcription into the Gematria Primus Translator. For unknown pages, use Frequency Analysis and Text Analysis to inspect rune distribution, repeated fragments, word lengths, and likely separators. When testing shifts or keyed polyalphabetic ideas, try the Caesar Cipher, Atbash Cipher, and Vigenere Cipher pages with the Gematria Primus alphabet or its Latin value sequence. Earlier Cicada stages also make OutGuess Extractor and Binary Analysis useful for checking images and recovered byte streams.

The examples below reproduce known community methods. They are useful as calibrations: if your transcription, alphabet order, or shift direction is wrong, these examples will fail before you ever reach the unsolved pages.

Welcome

Source identifiers: 03.jpg and 04.jpg. This page can be worked through with the Vigenere Cipher tool, but it is not ordinary Vigenere across the full page. Liber Primus skips advancing the key position at certain ciphertext indices having the letter ᚠ, known as the F(ᚠ)-skip rule. Those skips break the regular repeating-key rhythm, so start the autosolver with a block that has no F-skips. The first three lines work well for that calibration and recover the key DIVINITY (ᛞᛁᚢᛁᚾᛁᛏᚣ).

  1. Open the Vigenere Cipher tool, choose autosolver mode, and select Gematria Primus.
  2. Paste only the first three lines of the page transcription, where the key stream is still regular, and run the autosolver to recover the key ᛞᛁᚢᛁᚾᛁᛏᚣ.
  3. Switch to manual mode, enter the full rune transcription in Input and the rune key ᛞᛁᚢᛁᚾᛁᛏᚣ, select decrypt.
  4. To compensate for the Liber Primus F-skip rule, remove the letter ᚠ at those ciphertext positions where the decryption gets out of sync (for instance change ᚠᚠ on line 3 to ᚠ).

An End

Source identifier: 56.jpg in LP2, shown as complete-set page 73.jpg in scream314/cicada3301. The known stream is phi(prime), equivalent to prime-minus-one for each prime, applied modulo 29 as a forward Gematria shift. This is not a simple Vigenere autosolve; it is a custom numerical stream that Boxentriq helps inspect and verify.

Use the Cicada 3301 Totient Cipher tool to reproduce this prime-minus-one workflow directly with the Liber Primus F-skip rule.

  1. Convert runes to Gematria positions.
  2. Generate the prime-minus-one stream and reduce each value modulo 29.
  3. Shift down in the forward Gematria alphabet and verify the result.

Known Clues

  • The verified 2016 message says that Liber Primus is the way, its words are the map, their meaning is the road, and their numbers are the direction.
  • Cicada's warning to verify OpenPGP signatures from 7A35090F is essential because many public claims are unsigned or impersonated.
  • Modulo 29 matters because Gematria Primus has 29 runes. Shifts, key streams, and prime-derived methods often reduce values into that alphabet.
  • Prime and totient patterns appear in solved material. The An End method uses phi(prime), which is prime-minus-one, as its stream.
  • Gematria sums and prime/emirp observations are useful checks, but they are supporting evidence, not a complete decryption method by themselves.
  • The An End page points to a deep-web page by hash, which keeps the unsolved work tied to both text and external retrieval.
  • Rune values are ambiguous: U/V, C/K, S/Z, NG/ING, and IA/IO has to be interpreted.
  • Unverified public solutions should be treated as proposed or community analysis until the method is reproducible and source-framed.
