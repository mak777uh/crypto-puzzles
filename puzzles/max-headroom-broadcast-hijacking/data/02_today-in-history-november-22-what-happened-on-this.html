Today in History - November 22: Nobody was on duty when TV broadcast was hijacked

Nick Pearson

November 21, 2025 - 8.02pm

Two Chicago television stations were interrupted by an alarming, profane and thoroughly odd pirate broadcast on November 22, 1987.

And decades later, the people responsible have never been identified.

During the 9pm news on WGN-TV, the sports segment cut out and viewers saw a man in a Max Headroom mask in front of a swirling corrugated metal panel.

Advertisement

After thirty seconds of the interruption, accompanied by a loud droning sound, WGN-TV regained control of the airwaves and the news bulletin continued.

The second interruption lasted much longer.

There was nobody on duty at the broadcast tower for WTTW.

As a result, people watching an episode of Doctor Who instead were shown a much longer broadcast.

The masked man made a series of nonsense remarks, quoted a Coca-Cola slogan and hummed part of the theme song to children's cartoon Clutch Cargo.

At the end of the 90 second interruption, a woman dressed as a French maid smacked the man's bare bottom with a fly swat.

Because nobody was at the broadcast tower to regain control, the interruption only ended because the mystery pranksters stopped.

Advertisement

Max Headroom was a popular fictional television character at the time.

While billed as being a computer-generated character, Max Headroom was actually an actor in prosthetic make-up who stuttered to feign a technical glitch.

The prank was only achieved through substantial know-how and technical capacity.

Those responsible must have sent a more powerful signal to the broadcast towers, from somewhere within the line of sight of the buildings.

But an investigation from law enforcement came to nothing because the pranksters were never found.

And enforcement body FCC threatened a $100,000 fine and a year in jail for the culprits - who to this day have not been found.
